## Reliability, secrets and operations

Railway/Render provide deployment logs, server-side variables, process restart and revision rollback with less Linux maintenance. Configure exactly one replica, the shared Dockerfile at repository root, no public domain/HTTP health route, and disable scale-to-zero. Keep `DATABASE_URL_WORKER` direct/TLS and all RPC keys in service secrets. Review billing limits and alert before enforced shutdown; a spending cap can trade availability for a hard budget. Payment verification and account acceptance are determined by each provider, not guaranteed here.

Ubuntu VMs run the same image under `deploy/linux/flownix-worker.service`: systemd restart-on-failure, nonroot container, read-only root, capped CPU/RAM/logs and no published ports. SSH host fingerprints must be verified; the private key stays local. A root-only `/etc/flownix/worker.env` is server-side configuration; no OAuth secrets are copied. VM operators own security updates, disk/CPU alerts, recovery and rolling back the image tag. Allow SSH only from trusted operator addresses and outbound HTTPS/PostgreSQL; no public worker API is needed. Restrict Docker daemon access because it is privileged. An alive process/lock is distinct from successful collection; inspect cursor lag and provider errors too.

AWS/Google add IAM, project/billing controls, disk/network SKUs and optional paid logging/NAT. Avoid creating a NAT gateway for this small worker. Do not assume deleting/stopping a VM removes billable disks, IPs or snapshots. New AWS free accounts use time-limited credits/plans; [eligibility depends on signup date](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/ec2-free-tier-usage.html). These are not unconditional perpetual free EC2 offers.

Oracle's currently published A1 allowance is 1,500 OCPU-hours and 9,000 GB-hours/month (2 OCPUs/12 GB equivalent), with 200 GB combined boot/block storage. A 1 OCPU/2 GB proposal fits only within the whole tenancy's eligible allocation and home region. Idle instances may be reclaimed after seven days under the published CPU/network/memory criteria. Account approval/capacity remain blockers. Do not generate artificial activity to defeat reclamation or upgrade automatically.

## Neon is a separate operating limit

[Neon's published Free plan](https://neon.com/pricing) currently includes 100 CU-hours/month and 1 GB storage/project. The account's Vercel marketplace contract/usage dashboard is authoritative. At an assumed continuously active 0.25 CU, 730 hours means **182.5 CU-hours**, exceeding that allowance. Frequent writes/heartbeats can prevent scale-to-zero. A cheap/free worker host does not resolve this. Verify actual Neon usage and obtain approval before any plan change; no upgrade has been made. Storage is currently around 71 MiB and the worker halts at its configurable 750 MB guard without deleting research. That guard is not a Neon compute/billing cap.

Measured storage optimization: the latest 686 registry-market rows average **476 bytes**, down from the earlier **1,743 bytes** (~73% smaller), with all 686 hash references resolving to retained exact source responses. Full token metadata is no longer repeated in every normalized price row; original responses/membership evidence remain. At 686 rows every 15 minutes, even 476 bytes × 686 × 96 × 30 is **~0.94 GB/month before indexes, source snapshots, candles and wallet history**. Thus the current Free storage allowance cannot be assumed to support months of full-fidelity collection; the 750 MB guard can halt much earlier. No records were deleted. Current total database size is about **71 MiB**.

Reduce cost by incremental candles, bounded history pages, batched parameterized inserts, reused connections, cached/factory-proven pools, sequential scans and bounded receipt budgets. Next optimize shared block lookups/log batches and incremental wallet materialization after proving safe cursor coverage. Review a lossless archive/storage strategy or an explicitly approved Neon plan when needed; do not pretend a worker-host choice solves research storage. Do not lower monitoring fidelity or let jobs sleep merely to fit a headline price. No persistent local volume or second PostgreSQL instance is necessary.
